Where Private Wells Are Still Common

If your water pressure has been dropping off gradually, you might have written it off as normal wear. It's usually not — it's a well pump telling you it's working harder than it should, before it stops working at all.

Most of Bonita Springs runs on Bonita Springs Utilities water service. Some older neighborhoods near the Imperial River still rely on private wells. So do inland properties in areas like Golden Gate. These systems need their own care, separate from anything tied to the central utility network.

What Happens If the Pump Fails Completely

A well pump doesn't usually fail without warning — but once it does, there's no water at all until it's fixed.

No water means no water anywhere

Unlike a municipal connection, a failed well pump means zero water pressure across the entire home, not just one fixture.

A struggling motor can burn out

A pump cycling excessively or running against reduced flow puts real strain on the motor, shortening its remaining life.

Emergency repairs cost more

A same-day emergency call for total pump failure typically costs more than a scheduled repair caught early.

Common Signs of a Failing Well Pump

Reduced water pressure across the house is usually the first thing homeowners notice.

  • Water that sputters or runs air before flowing steadily
  • A pump cycling on and off more frequently than normal
  • A jump in electricity usage without an obvious cause
  • All worth having checked before the pump fails completely

Hard Water and Sediment From the Local Aquifer

Private wells here draw from the same rock layer behind the area's usually hard water. Mineral buildup and some sediment are a normal part of well ownership. Older wells that haven't had recent care tend to show discoloration and reduced flow more often. A repair visit is a good time to check whether filtration would help too.

Patterns We See on Calls Like This

Common with gradual pressure loss

A frequent call is a homeowner who noticed pressure dropping over several weeks and assumed it was normal aging. In many of these cases, the pump is cycling far more than it should to compensate, and catching it at this stage usually means a straightforward repair. Waiting until the pump fails completely often means an emergency call and, in some cases, a full pump replacement instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are signs a well pump is failing?

Reduced water pressure is one sign. So is water that sputters or runs air before flowing normally. The pump cycling on and off often, or a jump in electricity usage, are common signs too.

Why do wells in this area sometimes produce hard or discolored water?

Private wells draw from the same rock layer that makes the area's water hard overall. Mineral content and some discoloration from sediment are common. Especially in older wells that haven't been serviced in a while.

How long do well pumps often last?

Most well pumps last eight to fifteen years depending on usage, water quality, and pump type. Regular maintenance can extend that range, while heavy sediment or mineral content can shorten it.
