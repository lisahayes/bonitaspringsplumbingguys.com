Signs Your Home's Pipes Are Aging Out

If you've had more than one pinhole leak in the last year, you're probably starting to wonder if it's ever going to stop. It's a fair question, and the honest answer is often no — not until the actual pipe material is dealt with, not just the latest leak.

Recurring pinhole leaks in different spots over a short period is the clearest sign. Discolored or rusty water, especially right after the water's been off for a while, points to internal pipe corrosion. Persistently low pressure across multiple fixtures, not just one, and visibly green or corroded exposed copper are the other main indicators that it's time to think about the whole system instead of another one-off repair.

What Happens If You Keep Chasing Individual Leaks

Patching leak after leak on a corroding system costs more than it looks like on any single invoice.

Each repair costs more than the last

Individual pinhole repairs add up fast — several $250-400 fixes in a year often exceed what a planned repipe would have cost.

The next leak is unpredictable

Corroded copper doesn't fail on a schedule. The next pinhole could be behind a finished wall instead of somewhere visible.

Water damage risk compounds

Every additional leak is another chance for water to sit unnoticed long enough to cause real structural damage.

Why This Comes Up More in Older Bonita Springs Neighborhoods

Older, river-adjacent neighborhoods like Imperial Shores were often built with copper supply lines that are now decades old, the same era as the cottages around downtown's Riverside Park. Salt air corrosion and hard, limestone-aquifer water both accelerate the internal and external wear on copper over time. This means homes here can hit the "repipe territory" mark sooner than the same age home in a different climate. Newer builds already on PEX, like most homes in Bonita Bay, usually don't face this issue for many years.

Copper-to-PEX Conversion

Most repipes here involve converting from original copper to PEX piping, which resists the corrosion issues that shortened the original lines' lifespan in the first place.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my home needs a full repipe?

Recurring pinhole leaks, discolored water, persistently low water pressure across multiple fixtures, and visibly corroded exposed pipe are the main signs. One or two of these on their own might just need a repair, but several together usually point to the whole system aging out.

How long does a whole house repipe take?

Most residential repipes take a few days, depending on home size and how much wall or slab access is needed. We'll give you a specific timeline once we've assessed the home.

Will I be without water during the repipe?

We work in sections where possible to minimize downtime, but there will be periods without water while old lines are disconnected and new ones connected. We'll walk you through the schedule before work starts.
